<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I lose my BTC when I sell a covered call?">
    No. Options are **cash-settled in USDT** — your BTCB never leaves your subaccount. If BTC finishes below the strike, nothing happens: you keep your BTCB and the premium. If BTC finishes above the strike, your subaccount is debited the difference `(settlement price − strike) × size` in USDT; if your cash balance doesn't cover it, the shortfall becomes a borrow against your BTCB collateral. Economically you've sold your upside above the strike — but you never lose custody of the BTCB itself.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="When do I receive the premium?">
    Immediately, at trade execution. The premium moves from the maker's subaccount to yours **in the same atomic transaction** that transfers the option. There is no escrow period and no waiting for expiry.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is this custodial? Can the operator take my funds?">
    Your balances live in a subaccount NFT held by the Matching contract, with your address recorded as owner. Every state change requires **your EIP-712 signature**, and each signature authorizes exactly one narrowly scoped operation. The trade executor cannot change prices or sizes, redirect funds, or execute anything twice. If the operator vanishes, you can withdraw your subaccount permissionlessly: `requestWithdrawAccount` → wait 30 minutes → `completeWithdrawAccount`, after which you control the NFT directly on-chain.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What exactly does my signature authorize?">
    One trade: *this* instrument, at *this* price and size, settled into *your* subaccount, executed at most once, before an expiry timestamp you chose. The contracts enforce all of these bounds (`RFQM_SignedAccountMismatch`, `RFQM_InvalidTakerHash`, `RFQM_FeeTooHigh`, nonce invalidation, `OV_ActionExpired`). See the [EIP-712 reference](/reference/eip-712).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Why was the fee on the first testnet trade so large?">
    The original deploy shipped a **placeholder** open-interest fee rate of 10% of forward notional per side, which produced a 6,279 USDT fee against a 378.59 USDT premium on the first live trade. It has since been fixed on-chain: the rate is now **0.1% of notional with a 10 USDT floor, per side** — the same trade today would pay ~62.79 USDT per side. The rate remains governance-settable, so read the live values on-chain rather than hard-coding them — see [Fees](/reference/fees).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I close or cancel a position before expiry?">
    Not in v1. Options are European and there is no buy-back/unwind flow yet — once your covered call is sold, the position runs to expiry. For makers, a quote can be [cancelled or replaced](/market-makers/wire-protocol#replace-and-cancel) while its auction is still open, but the underlying signature can only age out at its expiry timestamp, and a won auction is locked in until the taker-accept deadline. Plan strikes, expiries, and quote TTLs accordingly.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if no market maker quotes my RFQ?">
    The auction window closes with zero quotes and the RFQ status becomes `expired`. Nothing executes, nothing is charged, and your collateral is untouched. You can simply open a new RFQ.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do market makers need permission to participate?">
    Yes — the engine enforces a **maker allowlist**: register your maker address with the team, and you can then authenticate with the signature handshake and quote from a funded subaccount under Matching. Un-allowlisted addresses are refused at WebSocket auth (close code `4003`). On-chain setup itself (subaccount, deposits) is permissionless. See [Onboarding](/market-makers/onboarding).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Are puts, other assets, or buying options supported?">
    Not yet. v1 is **sell-direction covered calls on BTC only** — the engine rejects any other direction. Cash-secured puts, more underlyings, and margin-traded (cash-settled) options are on the [roadmap](/resources/roadmap).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Where do the prices used for margin and settlement come from?">
    BTC **spot** comes from a **Pyth adapter** (Pyth BTC/USD on BSC testnet) cross-checked against Chainlink — a deviation above 1% zeroes the price's confidence and trips the margin system's oracle-contingency penalty. Per-expiry forwards, the SVI vol surface, rates, and the USDT/USD feed remain on-chain **signed feeds**; on testnet these are posted by a 1-of-1 signer with a flat 60% IV surface — a known, testnet-only trust assumption — and **settlement is still priced off the signed forward print**. Details in [Oracles & feeds](/reference/oracles).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is the protocol audited?">
    The contracts are a pinned fork of open-source protocol contracts whose base was **audited by Sigma Prime** (public reports). The fork is deployed with the vendored source kept read-only; Hedge' own changes (deploy scripts, configuration, off-chain services) are the target for differential review. No independent audit of the Hedge deployment has been completed yet — it's testnet for a reason.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
