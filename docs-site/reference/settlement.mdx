All v1 instruments are **European options, cash-settled in USDT** at expiry. The underlying BTCB is never delivered: it stays in the writer's subaccount as collateral throughout.

## The sequence

<Steps>
  <Step title="Expiry passes">
    Until the settlement price is fixed, expired positions simply sit in their subaccounts. Nothing is at risk of "exercise by surprise" — settlement is deterministic from the on-chain fix.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The settlement price is fixed">
    At/after expiry, the forward feed receives signed settlement data fixing the expiry price. On testnet the fix comes from the feed's settlement-window TWAP aggregates posted by the feed signer; there is no external settlement auction yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Anyone settles, per account">
    Once the fix is posted, **anyone** may call `StandardManager.settleOptions(optionAsset, subaccountId)` for any account — settlement is permissionless. The protocol's ops service runs this routinely for known accounts, but you never depend on it for your own.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The payoff

For a call with strike `K`, settlement fix `F_settle`, and size `size`:

| Position | Cash movement at settlement |
|---|---|
| Long call | `+ max(F_settle − K, 0) × size` |
| Short call (covered writer) | `− max(F_settle − K, 0) × size` |

- **OTM** (`F_settle ≤ K`): the option expires worthless; the writer keeps BTCB collateral and the premium received at trade time.
- **ITM** (`F_settle > K`): the writer's subaccount is debited the intrinsic value in USDT. If that exceeds their cash balance, the difference is a borrow against their BTCB collateral within their margin limits — economically equivalent to having sold the upside above the strike.

The premium itself is **not** part of settlement — it was paid upfront when the trade executed.
