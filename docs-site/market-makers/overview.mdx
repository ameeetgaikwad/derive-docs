This section is for **integration engineers at trading firms** running quoting bots against the Hedge retail covered-call flow.

<Note>
**Status:** live on BSC testnet (chain ID **97**). Mainnet (BSC, 56) is not yet deployed. Everything in this guide is derived from the running system; where a capability is missing it is marked **not yet implemented** rather than speculated.
</Note>

## What you are buying

The v1 product is **fully collateralized BTC covered calls sold by retail takers to market makers via RFQ**:

- Retail users ("takers") deposit BTCB into a subaccount and sell European BTC call options against it. You, the maker, **buy** those calls and pay the premium in USDT.
- v1 RFQ direction is `"sell"` only — the taker always sells, the maker always buys. Puts and maker-sells are planned but **not yet implemented** (the engine rejects any other direction with `v1 supports direction "sell" only (covered calls sold to makers)`).
- Instruments are **European, cash-settled-at-expiry** BTC options, collateralized by physical BTCB on the taker side; premiums and settlement in USDT (the protocol's cash asset).
- All protocol amounts use **18 decimals** (BTCB and BSC USDT are natively 18-decimal tokens).
- Instrument naming: `BTC-<YYYYMMDD>-<strike>-C|P`, e.g. `BTC-20260619-69000-C`.

## The auction model

Every RFQ is a short **sealed-bid, first-price auction**:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Broadcast">
    The RFQ engine broadcasts the RFQ to **all authenticated makers** over WebSocket.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Sealed bidding window">
    Quotes are collected for a fixed window (`AUCTION_WINDOW_MS`, default **3,000 ms**; the live smoke test ran 8,000 ms). Competitor quotes are **not revealed** during the window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Winner selection">
    The **highest per-unit premium** wins; ties are broken by earliest arrival. First-price: if you win, you pay exactly the premium you quoted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Taker accept & atomic execution">
    The taker has a fixed window to accept the winning quote (`TAKER_ACCEPT_DEADLINE_MS`, default **120 s** after close — the winner is told the deadline). On accept, the engine (a registered *trade executor*) submits a single `Matching.verifyAndMatch` transaction pairing your signed maker order with the taker's signed accept. Option and cash legs move atomically inside the `SubAccounts` ledger; margin checks run in the same transaction. If the taker misses the deadline, the RFQ expires and the winning maker is notified and released.
  </Step>
</Steps>

There is no off-chain settlement, novation, or counterparty credit — **your signed EIP-712 order *is* the trade.**

## RFQ lifecycle

```text
open ──window ends, ≥1 quote──> closed ──taker accepts──> executing ──mined ok──> executed
  │                                │                           └──reverted/error──> failed
  │                                └──accept deadline passes──> expired (maker released, rfq_expired)
  └──window ends, 0 quotes──> expired
```

## What you integrate with

| Component | What it is |
|---|---|
| **RFQ engine** | WS/REST auction service + on-chain executor. Your bot connects to its maker WebSocket channel. |
| **Reference maker bot** | A complete reference implementation (auth, pricing, signing, quoting) — adapt it, or use it as a conformance reference for your own stack. |
| **TypeScript SDK** (`@hedge/shared`) | Signing and encoding library: EIP-712 Actions, RFQ order encoding, chain clients. |
| **Protocol contracts** | Matching + RfqModule + SubAccounts + StandardManager + feeds. Addresses in [Contracts & addresses](/reference/contracts). |
