# A portfolio can be active without becoming speculative

Most financial products begin with an instrument: a token, an option chain, a lending pool, or an exchange. Hedge begins with a holder and a question.

**What outcome would make this asset useful to you over the next week, month, or quarter?**

A long-term holder may be willing to sell some upside above a chosen price. Another may want to buy only below a chosen price. Those intentions exist before the holder learns the language of calls, puts, deltas, or volatility.

## The mismatch

Today, holders often face three blunt choices:

- Keep the asset idle and wait.
- Sell now, even if the preferred price has not arrived.
- Enter a professional trading interface and translate a personal goal into derivatives terminology.

The product gap is not simply access to options. It is **translation**: turning an outcome a holder can state into a bounded trade that a professional market maker can price.

<Note>
Hedge does not remove the trade-off. A covered call exchanges upside above the selected target for a premium. The product succeeds only when that trade-off is visible before acceptance.
</Note>

## The category

Hedge calls this an **outcome-driven market for held assets**. The holder expresses a target. The market prices the target. The protocol enforces the agreed outcome.

This framing matters because it changes the product surface:

- Start with a goal, not a trading instrument.
- Show both expiry outcomes, not only the premium.
- Bring professional price competition behind a simple request.
- Keep execution and risk rules consistent as the catalog expands.

The first product is narrow by design. The larger thesis is that many held assets contain intentions that can become markets.
